\abstract{We audit whether the released SLRTP2025 back-translation (BT) evaluator for sign language production can be reconstructed from its public artifacts, distributed as an evaluation-only repository without training scripts. Our reconstruction attempt failed.

Fourteen recipe-conditioned reconstructions reach development-set BLEU 6.5--9.4 versus the released 13.38, teacher-forced token accuracy 55--60\% versus 96.5\%, and training-pool readout 7.2--9.3 versus 78.8 (70.7\% exact match). A small-sample overfit test rules out gross failures in loading, masking, and decoding but does not validate the full-scale training implementation. No constructible checkpoint reproduces a constructed whole-donor replay-probe reversal observed under the released evaluator ($+10.24$ sacreBLEU; post-selection descriptive interval). All constructible checkpoints are less competent, so the non-reproduction may reflect the competence gap, an unrecovered training mechanism, or their interaction.

Reference replacement with human sign-to-text back-translations reduces the gap to $+0.95$ (from $+10.03$) on a matched 461-item subset, a single-annotator sensitivity result. Donor-pool substitution reverses the sign.

These findings evidence a failed public-artifact reconstruction attempt, not a causal checkpoint effect, an identified memorization mechanism, or general leaderboard instability.}

\keywords{sign language production, back-translation evaluation, reproducibility audit, reference-frame sensitivity, learned evaluation metrics, PHOENIX-2014T}

\section{Introduction}

Sign language production (SLP) systems are commonly evaluated by back-translation (BT): a trained sign recognizer decodes each output pose sequence, and a text metric compares the decoding with the reference. This measures recognizer-readable content through a learned measurement model, so system rankings may reflect both the evaluated outputs and the inductive biases of a particular evaluator checkpoint. The present audit is framed as a \emph{public-recipe sufficiency audit}: whether the released SLRTP2025 BT evaluator and its replay-probe reversal can be reproduced from the publicly documented training recipe. We address three research questions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[RQ1.] Can the public recipe reproduce the released evaluator's competence and training-pool readout?
\item[RQ2.] Does a frozen replay stress probe's ordering reproduce across evaluator checkpoints constructed from the public recipe?
\item[RQ3.] How sensitive is the stress-probe reversal to the reference text and to donor origin?
\end{enumerate}

We do \emph{not} frame the work as a test of checkpoint identity, nor as a direct test of the Saunders et al.~\cite{saunders2021continuous} rank-stability proposition, which is conditioned on similar model configurations: every checkpoint we can construct from the public recipe is less competent than the released evaluator (14 paper-derived reconstructions: dev BLEU-4 6.5--9.4; broader constructible family max 9.8 under the uniform beam-3 protocol; training-log max 10.02; all vs.\ released 13.38), so competence and checkpoint identity are confounded. We therefore frame RQ2 strictly as a \emph{public-reconstruction non-reproduction} result: among independently constructed but weaker checkpoints, the ordering does not reproduce; the cause may be the competence gap, an unrecovered training mechanism, or their interaction.

Prior work shows BT scores can be modest for recorded signing and can improve without faithful target conditioning \cite{walsh2024datadriven,hong2026conditional}; sign-native or pose-aware metrics provide complementary evidence \cite{signbleu,silverscore,jiang2025meaningful}, yet BT remains central to SLRTP2025 \cite{slrtp2025}. Chen et al.~\cite{chen2022repro} showed that learned BERT-based evaluation metrics can fail to reproduce due to undocumented preprocessing, missing code, and baseline implementation differences---a methodology directly relevant to our finding that our public-artifact reconstruction attempt failed to reproduce the released checkpoint. An unresolved question is whether a retrieval--reference stress-test ordering persists across independently trained instances of the same BT architecture.

This question is exposed by a retrieval--reference reversal on PHX-public: under the released evaluator, source-conditioned whole-donor retrieval scores 23.02 sacreBLEU (0--100 scale) versus 12.78 for the recorded test poses---which neither establishes better signing nor invalidates BT evaluation in general, but asks whether the ordering depends on properties of the released evaluator that the public recipe does not recover.

We address the three research questions with the following evidence. For RQ1: fourteen reconstructions reach dev free-decode BLEU 6.5--9.4 (vs.\ released 13.38) and training-set teacher-forced token accuracy 55--60\% (vs.\ 96.5\%); a small-sample overfit test (BLEU 100.0 on 50--100 samples) rules out gross loading, masking, and decoding failures but does not validate the full-scale training implementation. For RQ2: the 14 reconstructions give a seed-level prediction interval of $[-1.94,-0.46]$; 24 heterogeneous-family checkpoints extend the range to $[-2.01,-0.21]$. For RQ3: on the matched 461-item subset, replacing original references with human sign-to-text back-translations reduces the gap from $+10.03$ to $+0.95$ (not distinguishable from zero, paired item CI $[-0.13, 1.99]$); donor-pool substitution reverses its sign. The probe's source-conditioned retriever shares surface vocabulary with the original reference (\S\ref{sec:retrieval}), so attenuation reflects both reference-text properties and disruption of this lexical coupling. The released evaluator's training-pool readout of 78.8 BLEU (70.7\% EM) far exceeds any constructible checkpoint (family max 9.3).

\paragraph{Why a constructed probe matters.}
Retrieval-augmented SLP entries draw donors from the evaluator's training pool, so the probe isolates a risk that production systems face in diluted form; methodologically, the audit contributes a transferable template---provenance table, checkpoint registry with SHA-256 deduplication, evaluator-replication controls, and teacher-forced-vs-free-decode diagnostics---for public-recipe reproducibility testing; the specific reversal is a PHX-public released-evaluator case study and does not support general claims about BT evaluation or retrieval-based SLP.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Learned evaluation in sign language production.}
Neural SLP often evaluates with a frozen sign recognizer \cite{camgoz2018neural,saunders2020progressive}; BT was proposed as an SLP evaluation protocol by Saunders et al.~\cite{saunders2021continuous}, who argued it shifts absolute scores but not relative rankings between similar configurations --- a proposition our audit does not directly test (its similarity condition is unmet in our family) but re-examines for a released evaluator on which a retrieval-probe reversal appears. SignBLEU \cite{signbleu}, SiLVERScore \cite{silverscore}, and pose-native measures offer complementary views, but BT BLEU remains central to SLRTP2025 \cite{slrtp2025}. Although newer metrics provide better human-correlated evaluation \cite{jiang2025meaningful,backtranslation2}, the SLRTP2025 BT evaluator is the \emph{standardized infrastructure} on which the challenge ranking depends; auditing its reproducibility and failure modes is infrastructure maintenance, not a claim that decoded BT-BLEU is the best available metric.

\paragraph{Retrieval and shortcut risks.}
Retrieval-based SLP ranges from phonetic composition to retrieval-enhanced generation \cite{walsh2024datadriven,signstitching,soke,darslp}, and SLRTP2025's leading entry is retrieval-based, making donor copying pertinent \cite{slrtp2025}. Memorization results in retrieval-augmented language modeling show strong task scores can reflect reproduction of training examples \cite{carlini2021extracting,kandpal2022memorization,khandelwal2020knnlm}; a shortcut under one learned evaluator does not show the ordering persists under another checkpoint.

\paragraph{Robustness of learned metrics and corpus audits.}
Hong and Ko\v{s}eck\'{a}~\cite{hong2026conditional} show BT-BLEU can diverge from target faithfulness; Walsh et al.~\cite{walsh2024datadriven} document a low recorded-pose BT baseline; Jiang et al.~\cite{jiang2025meaningful} recommend BT likelihood over decoded BLEU; and model-based generation metrics are further criticized by \cite{yazdani2025critical,backtranslation2,he2023blind}. Two 2026 PHOENIX-2014T audits bear directly on our reversal: Czehmann et al.~\cite{czehmann2026sacred} released human back-translations and documented reference information loss, Alkain et al.~\cite{alkain2026critical} showed the highest train--test text overlap among tested SLT corpora, and Artiaga et al.~\cite{artiaga2025signer} showed signer-dependent evaluation overestimates SLT capability. None runs multi-checkpoint evaluator experiments or donor-pool substitution controls.

\paragraph{Reproducibility of learned evaluation metrics.}
Chen et al.~\cite{chen2022repro} showed that reproducing BERT-based evaluation metrics can fail due to undocumented preprocessing, missing code, and baseline implementation differences, even when model weights are released---a finding methodologically close to our public-recipe non-reproduction result. The difference is that our audit targets a full BT evaluator pipeline (architecture, training recipe, and checkpoint) rather than a metric alone, and the non-reproduction is demonstrated across 38 non-degenerate independently trained instances.

\paragraph{PHOENIX-2014T as a language resource: known limitations.}
PHOENIX-2014T~\cite{camgoz2018neural} is the most widely used parallel corpus for German Sign Language (DGS) production and translation, containing 8,257 weather-forecast interpretations by nine signers aligned with German speech transcriptions. Its broad adoption makes auditing its evaluation infrastructure relevant to the language-resource community, but several properties constrain the audit's generalizability. First, the corpus is template-dense: weather forecasts reuse a restricted vocabulary and stereotyped syntactic patterns, inflating n-gram overlap between training and test sets~\cite{alkain2026critical}. Our stratified analysis confirms this: the 32 items whose reference exactly matches a training text account for a disproportionate share of the corpus-level gap. Second, the reference texts are speech transcriptions, not translations of the signing. They capture spoken German, not what the signer expressed in DGS. Czehmann et al.'s human back-translations quantified the resulting information loss~\cite{czehmann2026sacred}. Third, the SLRTP2025 pose materialization represents each signer as a 178-keypoint upper-body skeleton (42 hand, 128 face, 8 body keypoints), which discards the video-channel handshape and non-manual detail available in the original PHOENIX-2014T recordings; our supplementary MSKA control set uses a different 133-keypoint format (SI~Sup.~L). Fourth, the signer pool (9 signers) is small, and signer-dependent evaluation can overestimate translation capability~\cite{artiaga2025signer}.

These limitations are not unique to PHOENIX-2014T; many sign language corpora share template density, speech-transcribed references, and limited signer diversity. The specific lexical coupling between source and reference (the German sentence is both input and evaluation target) amplifies their effect in our stress probe. Section~\ref{sec:method} documents our provenance controls; the CSL-Daily boundary check (SI~Sup.~L) tests whether the readout pattern generalizes to a non-German corpus with different structural properties. The transferable components of our audit template (provenance table, checkpoint registry, leakage controls) are designed to apply to any learned evaluator paired with a publicly released training corpus.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Evaluation design}
Let $E_c$ denote a BT evaluator checkpoint, $x_n$ the source sentence (German) for item $n$, and $o_{i,n}$ the pose sequence for output condition $i$ and item $n$; $E_c$ produces $h_{c,i,n}=E_c(o_{i,n})$ and $B_{c,i}=M(\{(h_{c,i,n},y_n)\}_{n=1}^{N})$, where $y_n$ is the reference text and $M$ is the corpus-level sacreBLEU functional unless stated otherwise. We call a result \emph{evaluator-pipeline-sensitive} when a substantive pairwise ordering changes across independently trained checkpoints under a fixed output set. The fixed primary set comprises recorded test poses (REC), random donor copy, source-conditioned whole-donor retrieval, a Progressive Transformer generator, and retrieval--generator compositions; oracle-gloss variants are diagnostic controls.

\paragraph{Operational definition of public-recipe sufficiency.}
\label{sec:sufficiency_def}
A publicly documented training recipe is \emph{sufficient} for reproducing a released evaluator when checkpoints constructed from it reach a predefined competence gate (our \emph{operational tolerance}, not a community standard): $|\Delta\text{dev BLEU-4}|\leq\delta_{\text{BLEU}}$ and $|\Delta\text{dev WER}|\leq\delta_{\text{WER}}$, with $\delta_{\text{BLEU}}{=}1.0$, $\delta_{\text{WER}}{=}3.0$ (released dev values: 13.38 / 83.37). We justify these bounds as the range below which a reconstruction would shift the SLRTP2025 leaderboard's BLEU-4 by less than one rank position; the gate conclusion is insensitive across a $\pm[0.5,2.0]_{\text{BLEU}}\times\pm[1.5,4.5]_{\text{WER}}$ grid (\S\ref{sec:what-drives-reversal}). Insufficiency can arise from two sources. (i) \emph{Information incompleteness:} the public artifacts omit training details (data preprocessing specifics, learning-rate schedule shape, validation-selection protocol, intermediate checkpoints) that the original training relied on. (ii) \emph{Stochastic underspecification:} a single rare seed or optimizer trajectory produced the released result. Our evidence favors (i): the competence shortfall is consistent across 14 seeds (dev BLEU-4 6.5--9.4 vs.\ 13.38; training-set teacher-forced token accuracy 55--60\% vs.\ 96.5\%), and local-robustness checks confirm the released checkpoint is not a fragile singular point (SI~Sup.~F). Without the original training command and intermediate checkpoints, the two sources cannot be fully separated.

The main evaluation uses PHX-public, a German weather-domain text-to-pose benchmark whose released SLRTP2025 pose records contain 7,060/515/641 sequences; one official test ID is absent from the released materialization, so every result is conditioned on the complete 641-record release (SI~Sup.~A). Table~\ref{tab:provenance} gives the three-way provenance: the official PHOENIX-2014T split, the released pose materialization, and the released evaluator's actual training manifest (confirmed via \texttt{config.yaml}, whose manifest matches the shipped \texttt{.pt} tensors). Supplementary analyses use \textbf{PHX-holdout-MSKA} (133-joint, 1,538 windows) and CSL-Daily (Mode B, 1,176 sequences; SI~Sup.~L); the protocol summary is in SI~Table~S28.

\subsection{Systems and retrieval controls}
\label{sec:retrieval}
For each target, source-conditioned whole-donor retrieval scans the complete training pool after Unicode NFKC normalization, whitespace normalization and lowercasing, selecting the sequence with maximum source-text token-set Jaccard similarity. Oracle-gloss retrieval instead uses target gloss and is treated as an input-leakage diagnostic; random retrieval selects a donor uniformly. Donor exclusion removes exact normalized text or gloss duplicates or donors above threshold $\tau$; gloss-based exclusion is therefore an offline diagnostic, not a deployable inference procedure.

\emph{Single canonical materialization.} Every number in this paper is generated from one canonical donor registry built by the deterministic \S\ref{sec:retrieval} algorithm (exact-normalized-text exclusion applied), whose SHA-256 is \texttt{9170a53026ab3263b451a3632a9e02\-318745acabf12f0b679921477afbefa301}. Reproduction is supported at four layers (\S~Sup.~Q, Table~S36): (i) \textbf{L1---number recomputation}: a single command (\texttt{make core-audit}) regenerates the registry and every headline number \emph{from the committed per-item decodes, not from training data}; (ii) \textbf{L2---analysis re-execution}: each analysis script re-executes from the committed per-checkpoint data and hypotheses; (iii) \textbf{L3---model re-evaluation}: re-decoding fixed outputs from checkpoint weights requires the training bundle; (iv) \textbf{L4---from-scratch retraining}: full retraining (\texttt{scripts/train\_reconstruction.py}, \texttt{launch\_reconstructions.sh}) requires the restricted PHOENIX-2014T licence, the exact environment, and approximately 200 GPU-hours. The \texttt{make core-audit} command executes L1 only and is not an end-to-end reproduction. An earlier pre-exclusion materialization (PURE 23.79, gap $+11.01$) survives only as a sensitivity analysis in SI~Sup.~N: 610/641 items (95.2\%) are identical across materializations, and a 14-reconstruction comparison gives mean $|\Delta\text{gap}| = 0.65$ BLEU (max 1.45), so qualitative conclusions are unchanged.

\paragraph{Lexical coupling of the source-conditioned probe.}
In PHX-public, the source sentence $x_n$ (German weather report) is identical to the reference text $y_n^{\text{orig}}$ at the sequence level (the reference is a speech transcription of the same German sentence). The TN-PURE-v1 retriever selects training-pool donors by token-set Jaccard similarity between $x_n$ and training texts, so it preferentially retrieves donors whose accompanying training texts share surface-level vocabulary with both the source \emph{and} the reference. Scoring a donor copy against this lexically coupled reference under a learned evaluator therefore confounds donor similarity, evaluator readout, and reference overlap. This coupling is deliberate in the stress-probe design---it isolates the strongest form of the shortcut---but it means the human-reference attenuation analysis (\S\ref{sec:human_refs}) reflects disruption of the lexical coupling as well as reference-text information loss, not a clean separation of the two.

\paragraph{Retrieval composition.}
\label{sec:composition}
TN-PURE-v1 uses whole-donor copy with [::2] subsampling to 12.5\,fps, preserving the donor's original duration. TN-PTCOMP-v1 (a secondary control) composes a PT scaffold with donor hands and face via $\alpha$-blending of upper-body joints ($\alpha{=}0.88$; full joint-set definitions in the artifact). The matched seen--unseen factorial retains 605/641 targets matched on duration and word count.

\paragraph{Donor-origin contrast retrieval systems.}
\label{sec:exposure_systems}
To test whether the retrieval advantage co-occurs with donor origin, we construct frozen probe systems from the same 641 queries: \textbf{UNSEEN-PURE-v1} retrieves from the 640 other test poses; \textbf{CROSSFIT-PURE-A/B-v1} split queries by hash into two folds, each retrieving from the other fold; \textbf{SEEN-PURE-MATCHED-v1} selects a train-pool donor within $\pm0.1$ Jaccard of each UNSEEN donor (622/641 admit one); \textbf{SEEN-RAND640-MATCHED-v1} applies matched selection inside frozen 640-donor sub-pools. SEEN-PURE-v1 re-uses the canonical TN-PURE-v1 registry; these are constructed probes, not deployable generators (construction rules in the artifact).

\subsection{Evaluator checkpoint family}
The primary inferential checkpoint family comprises the original frozen SLRTP2025 evaluator and six \emph{recipe-conditioned reconstruction} runs trained from scratch with the documented architecture on a common frozen partition (primary seeds 101--606; eight extension seeds 707--1405 use the identical protocol). The original bundle's \texttt{validations.txt} logs 202 validation points over 2{,}828 optimizer steps ($\approx$102 epochs), with decoded-BLEU selection reaching best dev BLEU-4 13.38 at step 1{,}820; the trajectory is analyzed in \S\ref{sec:original_trajectory}, and intermediate checkpoints are not published. We use two reconstruction protocols --- \emph{paper-derived} (14 runs, legacy script, 300 epochs, NLL selection) and \emph{released-config} (4 runs, up to 3{,}000 epochs, decoded-BLEU early stopping) --- sharing the documented architecture (3L/256/512ff, 8 heads), tokenizer, manifests (7{,}060/515), $[::2]$ subsampling, Adam (1e-3, wd .001), batch size 256, and plateau $\times0.8$ LR schedule (full provenance in SI). In total 70 runs across 13 families (registry in SI~Sup.~D); 43 were decoded on PHX-public under the canonical \S\ref{sec:retrieval} donor registry, corresponding to 42 unique weight binaries --- the long-schedule seed-202 artifact is byte-identical to the config-faithful seed-202 artifact (identical \texttt{config.yaml} and wandb run, identical per-step losses, identical best-checkpoint SHA-256), so it is a duplicate of that run rather than an independent replicate. Of the 43 decoded runs, five are degenerate (three $\alpha{=}1.0$ distillation students with empty hypotheses, two smallest ladder fractions with BLEU${}\approx 0$) and are excluded, so the non-replication count is 38 non-degenerate decoded runs (37 unique binaries), all with strictly negative gaps.

\paragraph{Training-pool readout (descriptive observation).}
\label{sec:readout_def}
We define \emph{training-pool readout} as the in-sample autoregressive free-decode BLEU and exact-match (EM) rate on the full 7{,}060-item training set under beam-3 decoding---not teacher-forced accuracy. The released evaluator decodes its full 7{,}060-item training pool at free-decode BLEU 78.8 with 70.7\% exact-match, while retaining dev/test performance of 13.38/12.78. The constructible family's readout is far lower (reconstructions: 7.2--9.3 BLEU, 1.5--2.3\% EM; distillation: 6.6--8.9 BLEU, 1.3--1.9\% EM); no constructible checkpoint approaches both simultaneously. The training-pool free-decode BLEU ratio (released: $\text{78.8}\,/\,\text{13.38}\approx5.9$ vs.\ reconstruction family: 0.93--1.12) quantifies how much the model reads out above its in-domain performance. We report these as continuous descriptive observations: the released evaluator was observed first, the family range afterwards, and any threshold is post-hoc.

\subsection{Reconstruction sanity checks}
\label{sec:sanity_checks}
Because all 70 training runs across 13 families share the same codebase, data preprocessing, and hyperparameters, we performed three checks to rule out a systematic implementation defect shared across all runs:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{Small-sample overfit test.}] We trained a reconstruction model on 50 (and separately 100) randomly sampled training sequences for $\approx$200 epochs with the identical pipeline. On the 50-sample set, teacher-forced NLL fell from 7.66 to 0.009 and free-decode sacreBLEU reached 100.00; on the 100-sample set, free-decode BLEU also reached 100.00. The reconstruction code can therefore overfit small data to near-perfect free-decode BLEU, ruling out a fundamental defect in checkpoint loading, tokenizer, padding-mask, EOS, loss normalization, or the beam-search decoding path (results: \texttt{results/overfit\_test.json}).

\item[\textbf{Unified inference regression.}] Released and reconstruction checkpoints are loaded through the identical \texttt{make\_back\_translation\_model()} function and decoded with the identical \texttt{back\_translate()} pipeline. All 15 checkpoints (released + 14 reconstructions) share byte-identical \texttt{txt.vocab} and \texttt{gls.vocab} files (confirmed by SHA-256). The released checkpoint decoded through this path reproduces the canonical REC value exactly (12.78 on 641 items; \texttt{results/inference\_regression.json}).

\item[\textbf{Teacher-forced vs.\ free-decode diagnostics.}] Table~\ref{tab:sanity_stats} reports per-checkpoint teacher-forced token accuracy, NLL per token, and free-decode BLEU on training and development sets. The released evaluator achieves 96.5\% training-set teacher-forced token accuracy and 78.8 free-decode training BLEU (full-pool, beam-3); the fourteen reconstructions achieve 55.0--59.6\% (mean 57.2\%) token accuracy and 7.2--9.3 (mean 8.35) free-decode training BLEU. The reconstructions achieve slightly lower development-set teacher-forced NLL (2.63--2.68 vs.\ released 3.24), consistent with less overfitting, but their teacher-forced token accuracy is comparable on dev (45.8--47.4\% vs.\ released 48.6\%). The gap between released and reconstructed free-decode BLEU is therefore present in both training and development data and is already visible in teacher-forced token-level metrics, not only in free-decoding.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Discovery timeline and pre-registration status}
\label{sec:discovery}
The $+10.24$ reversal was first observed on 2026-07-24 in the canonical materialization with exact-normalized-text exclusion (SHA-256 \texttt{9170a530}); an earlier pre-exclusion materialization (gap $+11.01$) had been noted on 2026-07-22. The six primary reconstruction seeds (101--606) were specified before their training results were inspected, and the eight extension seeds (707--1405) were added post-hoc after observing the six primary results. The Jaccard-based retriever, PURE donor-copy protocol, exact-normalized-text exclusion rule, and 641-query set were locked before reconstruction training began, after exploring approximately four retrieval strategies (Jaccard, LaBSE, multi-SBERT, oracle gloss) and three construction variants (pure copy, PT-composed, oracle-gloss-composed) on the released evaluator alone. No external pre-registration exists for the specific $+10.24$ probe; the six primary seeds constitute the only pre-specified confirmatory evidence, and all heterogeneous-family results are post-hoc descriptive exploration. Because the Jaccard pure-copy probe was selected after exploring multiple retrievers and construction variants on the same test set, its CI is conditional on the selection and should be read as descriptive, not confirmatory. The full registry of which family enters which claim is in SI~Sup.~C.

\subsection{Metrics and uncertainty}
The recorded test poses (REC) are decoded by each BT recognizer; we avoid the abbreviation ``ground truth'' because the recorded poses are inputs to a learned measurement model, not a semantic gold standard. We report corpus BLEU-4 on the 0--100 sacreBLEU scale as the primary metric \cite{post2018sacrebleu} (full signature: \texttt{BLEU|c|n:4|e:no|tok:13a|s:exp|v:2.5.1}); sentence-level statistics (pass-through, slot-F1) are reported separately on the 0--1 scale. BLEU-1, chrF, ROUGE-L, BERTScore \cite{zhang2020bertscore} (\texttt{bert-base-multilingual-cased}, no baseline rescaling, F1), and WER (jiwer 3.1.0 normalized) provide complementary decoded-text \emph{cross-metric consistency} checks within the same BT hypotheses---their consistent direction across evaluators is not independent semantic validation because all metrics share the identical decoded outputs \cite{yazdani2025critical}. BT likelihood and pose-DTWp provide non-decoded controls; a non-standard BLEURT substitute used only for a secondary reference-sensitivity check is documented, with its validation against canonical BLEURT-20 and its known bias, in SI~Sup.~O.

Uncertainty is quantified with donor-cluster bootstrap (primary, because queries reuse 565 unique pose donors across 641 queries; 10{,}000 resamples, seed 42, percentile 95\% CIs) and complemented by item-level and query$\times$donor two-way cluster bootstraps (SI~Sup.~G). A pairwise difference is called detectable when its interval excludes zero. A descriptive seed-level 95\% Student-$t$ prediction interval is reported for the recipe-conditioned reconstruction population; its interpretation is strictly ``given a future random seed under this identical legacy implementation,'' not a claim about all public-recipe implementations. The eight extension seeds are post-hoc additions and enter only this interval (never the six primary comparisons); splitting by pre-specification status, the six primary seeds give PI $[-2.48,-0.28]$ and the eight extension seeds $[-1.61,-0.54]$, all fourteen leave-one-seed-out folds keep the interval below zero, and a two-level seed$\times$item bootstrap (seeds resampled with replacement, then items within each drawn seed) gives CI $[-1.49,-0.93]$ (SI~Sup.~B). The PURE--REC decoded-sacreBLEU gap under the released evaluator is the \textbf{selected probe gap}, reported as a post-selection descriptive estimate (see the probe multiplicity table in \S\ref{sec:probe_multiplicity} and the discovery timeline in \S\ref{sec:discovery}); all other contrasts are secondary and are Holm-corrected within a defined family or explicitly labelled descriptive (SI~Sup.~C).

\subsection{Terminology: run, checkpoint, unique binary, family, evaluator}
Throughout, \emph{run} denotes a single training execution; \emph{checkpoint} denotes the selected model weights from that run; \emph{unique weight binary} counts distinct SHA-256 hashes of checkpoint files (the cf\_202 and ls\_202 runs produced byte-identical weights from the same wandb run); \emph{family} denotes a group of runs sharing a training protocol; and \emph{evaluator} denotes a checkpoint used to decode fixed outputs. The 14 paper-derived reconstruction runs use NLL selection on the legacy script; the 4 released-config runs use decoded-BLEU early stopping. Both share the documented architecture and hyperparameters (\S\ref{sec:method}).

\paragraph{Experimental stratification.}
\label{sec:stratification}
We classify every checkpoint family into four evidential tiers. \emph{Tier 1 (direct public-recipe reconstruction)}: 14 paper-derived $+$ 4 config-faithful $+$ 2 step-faithful $=$ 20 runs; these are the only runs that support the reconstruction-failure claim. \emph{Tier 2 (pre-specified with known bias)}: 2 confirmation runs. \emph{Tier 3 (post-hoc diagnostic/sensitivity)}: 12 rescue $+$ 15 distillation $+$ 4 ladder $+$ 2 long-schedule $+$ 4 large-arch $=$ 37 runs; these serve as mechanism exploration. \emph{Tier 4 (local perturbation from released weights)}: 9 A2 fine-tunes $+$ A1 Gaussian noise variants; these probe the vicinity of the released checkpoint, not the public recipe. Main conclusions rest on Tier 1 only; Tiers 2--4 are reported as triangulating evidence and must not be cumulated with Tier 1 to inflate the appearance of independent replication.

\paragraph{Readout \emph{with} generalization: the distinctive combination.}
\label{sec:insample}
The released evaluator decodes its full 7{,}060-item training pool at free-decode BLEU 78.8 with 70.7\% exact-match --- strong readout --- while retaining non-trivial dev/test performance (13.38/12.78). Under the identical full-pool, beam-3 free-decode protocol, the constructible family stays far below: 14 reconstruction seeds span train BLEU 7.2--9.3 (EM 1.5--2.3\%) at dev BLEU 6.5--9.4, and 12 non-degenerate distillation students span train BLEU 6.6--8.9 (EM 1.3--1.9\%) at dev BLEU 7.0--9.0 (training-time dev BLEU-4 under the original selection protocol reaches 10.9; SI~Table~S26, SI~Sup.~E). None of the 27 non-degenerate constructible checkpoints approaches 78.8/70.7\%, and the released evaluator's donor-content pass-through is a 2.4$\times$ outlier (ratio 3.09 vs.\ family 1.05--1.31). Across the 31 checkpoints with uniform full-pool readouts (30 constructible, plus the released evaluator), training-pool readout tracks decoded competence (Spearman $\rho{=}0.916$, $n{=}31$, $p{<}10^{-12}$; $\rho{=}0.873$ over the 27 non-degenerate constructible checkpoints) and is uncorrelated with the train-minus-dev overfit indicator ($\rho{=}0.040$) or with the PURE--REC gap ($\rho{=}{-}0.082$; full table in SI~Sup.~D): within the constructible family the readout behaves as a competence proxy rather than an overfit signature, and the released evaluator's readout-with-generalization combination is not explained by any within-family overfit--readout relationship.

\paragraph{Leakage sanity checks for the train-pool readout.}
\label{sec:leakage_checks}
The 78.8/70.7\% readout is not a trivial implementation artifact: five controls (SI~Sup.~M) rule out four shortcut classes --- teacher forcing (code-path audit confirms autoregressive beam search), output--reference misalignment (label permutation collapses BLEU from 78.6 to $\sim$1.0), pose-ignorant ID/cache lookup (input-side pose permutation with verified output equivariance collapses BLEU to $<$1; a same-signer held-out control reaches only 12.19 BLEU / 3.2\% EM on unseen poses), and narrow-text memorization (high exact-match across all 9 signers and unique texts). The checks do not identify the training mechanism but are consistent with pose-conditioned training-pool exposure.

\paragraph{Knowledge-distillation and competence extrapolation.}
\label{sec:distillation_ladder}
\label{sec:extrapolation}
Fifteen distillation students trained from the released evaluator as teacher ($\alpha\in\{0,0.25,0.5,0.75,1.0\}$, three seeds) all have negative gaps and do not transfer the readout pattern (SI~Table~S26). OLS/GP extrapolation from the 42 unique non-released binaries predicts negative gaps at dev 13.38, but non-linear behavior inside the unobserved $(9.8, 13.38)$ interval cannot be excluded (SI~Sup.~K).

\paragraph{Synthesis.}
Across the five descriptive comparisons, the released evaluator is the only checkpoint combining high training-pool readout (78.8 BLEU, 70.7\% EM) with retained in-domain performance (13.38/12.78). No reconstruction or distilled student reproduces either the $+10.24$ gap or this combination.

\paragraph{Rank stability (see SI~Sup.~H).}
On a fixed nine-system stress-test set, the released evaluator ranks TN-PURE first; five of six reconstructions rank REC first, with moderate cross-checkpoint agreement (mean $\tau_b{=}0.44$, $\rho{=}0.57$).


\paragraph{Reference-frame sensitivity: matched-subset analysis.}
\label{sec:human_refs}
\label{sec:floor_effect}
Two 2026 corpus audits bear directly on this reversal. Czehmann et al.~\cite{czehmann2026sacred} released manual sign-to-text back-translations of the test set (one deaf fluent L2 DGS signer; per-item confidence flags), documenting information loss, lexical errors, and translationese in the original speech-transcribed references, and showed that reference replacement already changes BLEU scores and rankings on PHOENIX-2014T. Alkain et al.~\cite{alkain2026critical} quantified train--test text overlap across sign-language translation corpora, showing that a small fraction of highly training-like test items disproportionately inflates corpus BLEU. We reproduce the overlap pattern locally: the 32 test items whose reference exactly matches a training text score REC 40.26 / PURE 78.73 (gap $+38.5$), while the remaining 609 items score REC 11.93 / PURE 22.20 (gap $+10.3$).

Czehmann et al.\ compared original- and human-reference conditions on the same confidence=1 subset (462 of 642 items); we follow the same protocol. On the matched 461-item subset (after join with our 641 released test IDs), the original-reference gap is $+10.03$ BLEU points (REC 14.94 / PURE 24.98) and the human-reference gap is $+0.95$ (REC 7.35 / PURE 8.30): a paired attenuation of $9.08$ BLEU points (90.5\%, bootstrap CI $[7.44, 10.86]$ on the absolute attenuation and $[0.81, 1.01]$ on the attenuation ratio; SI~Table~S31; SI~Fig.~S5; per-item paired view in SI~Fig.~S2). The residual gap is not distinguishable from zero (paired item CI $[-0.13, 1.99]$). Teacher-forced BT likelihood does not reproduce the reversal under either reference (REC NLL $3.060$ vs PURE $3.479$ on original refs; $3.41$ vs $3.71$ on human refs) \cite{jiang2025meaningful}.

Scoring against both references jointly (closest-length convention) leaves the gap at $+11.50$, \emph{larger} than original. TN-PURE donors are selected by lexical overlap with the source sentence, and the original reference is a speech transcription of that same sentence, so adding it injects a second lexically coupled target the replay decode can match; the gap inherits the lexical coupling rather than averaging it away. The reversal is thus specific to reference frames containing the original speech-transcribed register. The full-641 comparison ($+10.24 \to +0.86$, $91.6\%$) mixes reference replacement with floor effects from low-confidence items.

This is a \emph{descriptive contrast of two corpus gaps}, not a causal attribution: the attenuation cannot distinguish reference-text defects, paraphrase/translationese shift, BLEU floor effects, disruption of the lexical coupling, or evaluator training-distribution alignment. Under all six reconstruction checkpoints the human-reference gap is negative ($-0.15$ to $-0.65$), and the asymmetry is consistent across metrics (chrF $82.2\%$: $+12.73 \to +2.27$; BLEU-1 $84.0\%$: $+20.01 \to +3.19$; SI~Table~S23), including a BLEURT-substitute check agreeing with Czehmann et al.\ (SI~Sup.~O). It quantifies how decoded-text scores shift when the reference frame changes, not whether the replay donor motion conveys target-equivalent meaning. The human back-translations are sign-conditioned paraphrases of the recorded test poses, not judgments of the replay donors, so the semantic adequacy of TN-PURE-v1 is left unassessed.

A natural concern is symmetric floor compression. Switching from original to human references, REC drops $7.59$ BLEU-4 points (50.8\%) but PURE drops $16.68$ (66.8\%) --- a larger fraction (ratio $1.31\times$), and the asymmetry is even larger under BLEU-1 and chrF (SI~Table~S23); simple symmetric compression is insufficient, though the asymmetry does not discriminate between reference-text defects and training-distribution alignment.

\subsection{Donor-origin contrast}
\label{sec:exposure_results}

If the released-evaluator reversal requires exposure to training-pool donors, retrieval restricted to unseen poses should remove or invert it: under the original evaluator, UNSEEN-PURE-v1 (retrieving from the 640 other test poses) scores 8.51 sacreBLEU vs.\ REC 12.78 (gap $-4.27$), a donor-pool substitution estimand of $+14.51$ BLEU points (SI~Table~S30). This is a \emph{donor-pool substitution effect}, not a pure exposure effect: SEEN and UNSEEN differ simultaneously in pool size (7{,}060 vs 640), median donor--reference Jaccard (0.50 vs 0.37), donor reuse (Gini 0.11 vs 0.29), and training-pool exposure. Cross-fit controls reproduce the direction (CROSSFIT pooled $-3.8$), and all reconstruction pool-origin contrasts lie in $[-0.15,+1.22]$.

\paragraph{Path-dependent decomposition and balanced factorial.}
\label{sec:decomposition}
\label{sec:factorial_corrected}
Telescoping-path decomposition (SI~Sup.~E) gives origin estimates of $+5.8$ to $+8.4$ BLEU across admissible orderings, because size and selection interact. The better-balanced 605-query factorial (matching seen/unseen donors at high/low LaBSE similarity) estimates the pool-origin main effect at $+2.08$ $[+1.56,+2.57]$, similarity at $+6.96$ $[+5.93,+8.08]$, and their interaction at $+3.70$ $[+2.63,+4.79]$ (all Holm $p{=}0.0003$); across reconstructions the seen main effect shrinks to $+0.51$ and the interaction is null ($-0.39$). Because seen donors remain more similar than unseen within strata (SMD $+0.4$), this is a train-pool vs test-pool provenance contrast, not an identified training-exposure effect.



\subsection{Competitive-system context}
\label{sec:competition_real}
Because the audit's stress-test set is constructed rather than competitive, the public SLRTP2025 leaderboard offers external context: computed under the same released evaluator, its ``Ground Truth'' row reproduces our canonical REC numbers exactly (BLEU-4 12.78, BLEU-1 34.40, WER 85.77 \cite{slrtp2025}; SI~Table~S25). The retrieval-based winner USTC-MoE sits roughly even with REC on the lexical BT metrics (BLEU-4 $-0.72$, BLEU-1 $+0.45$, chrF $+2.24$; WER 93.49 vs 85.77) while its DTW-MJE (0.0448) and duration ratio (1.438) indicate considerable pose-level deviation from recorded signing --- a single-evaluator co-occurrence that does not establish that the released evaluator favors retrieval entries; the two fixed-output cross-evaluator re-decoding studies below resolve this question directly.


\paragraph{Cross-evaluator re-decoding of realistic retrieval outputs.}
Per-team pose outputs are not archived, so we re-decoded two fixed outputs of realistic retrieval pipelines under all 44 evaluator checkpoints (the released evaluator plus the 43 reconstructed runs; per-evaluator cells in SI~Table~S32--S33). First, the \emph{noisy-gloss retrieval} pipeline, the deployment-realistic analogue of TN-PURE-v1, predicts each test sentence's gloss with a text-to-gloss NMT and retrieves the nearest training donor by predicted-gloss Jaccard (SI~Sup.~P). It decodes at 12.65 under the released evaluator versus 12.78 for REC (gap $-0.13$), reproducing the SI value: this realistic retrieval system is roughly even with recorded signing, unlike the constructed stress probe.

Second, we rebuilt the official winner's fixed test outputs from its released pipeline intermediates (text-to-gloss predictions, gloss-to-pose dictionary, official frame counts; 497 of 641 PHX-public keys; 471 retrieval and 26 copy branches; SI~Sup.~P). The rebuild decodes at 2.42 under the released evaluator (REC 12.36 on the same keys, gap $-9.94$), far below the officially reported leaderboard score of 12.06, so it is pipeline-faithful but not score-faithful to the archived submission. We report this as a \emph{failed reproduction attempt of the winner's fixed output}, not as evidence about the original competition entry.

Under the 43 reconstructed evaluators, every non-degenerate checkpoint gives a negative gap for both systems (noisy-gloss range $[-1.94,-0.07]$, mean $-1.18$; USTC-MoE range $[-8.30,-4.28]$, mean $-7.17$; the five degenerate checkpoints decode empty or near-empty hypotheses and give gaps of $0$). Neither realistic system recovers a retrieval-favoring ordering under any reconstructed evaluator, and the released evaluator does not favor them either (gaps $-0.13$ and $-9.94$). The $+10.24$ reversal is specific to the constructed whole-donor probe. We therefore separate two propositions: (i) \emph{Mechanism/stress-test proposition} (supported): the released evaluator is susceptible to a lexically-coupled whole-donor replay under a constructed probe. (ii) \emph{Leaderboard-impact proposition} (not supported by current data): no realistic retrieval system in our analysis reproduces a retrieval-favoring ordering under any evaluator, so we cannot claim that real competition rankings would change across checkpoints. As a benchmark-organizer responsibility, archiving fixed per-team outputs alongside public scores is the most direct and evidence-supported suggestion we can offer for enabling rank-stability audits by third parties.

\paragraph{Released-checkpoint local-robustness checks (experiments A1--A2, SI~Sup.~F).}
Two perturbation designs test whether the reversal is a fragile singular point versus a locally robust property near the released checkpoint. Experiment A1 adds i.i.d.\ Gaussian weight noise at increasing scale $\sigma$: the reversal is preserved within $\pm 0.75$ of $+10.24$ for $\sigma\leq 3\times10^{-3}$ (where dev BLEU-4 stays within $12.50$--$13.38$) and collapses in lockstep with competence at $\sigma\geq 10^{-2}$. Experiment A2 fine-tunes the released checkpoint with three seeds at small learning rates (5e-6--3e-5, 60 epochs each), moving weights along real gradient directions rather than isotropic noise; all nine variants preserve the reversal (gaps $+10.37$ to $+10.77$, dev BLEU-4 $12.92$--$13.18$, training-pool readout $80.5$--$81.8$ BLEU / $72.6$--$74.0\%$ EM). Both designs confirm the reversal is locally robust near the released weights --- not a fragile identity-level artifact --- but they probe only the vicinity of the released checkpoint and do not demonstrate that an independently trained, competence-matched model would exhibit the reversal, nor do they narrow the unobserved competence interval. Full sweep and figures in SI~Sup.~F.

\paragraph{Per-item gap decomposition (experiment C, SI~Sup.~K).}
An OLS regression ($R^2=0.33$, $n=641$) attributes the largest share of per-item gap variance to high-overlap-train count, template density, and donor--reference Levenshtein distance; signer identity contributes $\leq 1\%$. Full model, coefficient table, and figure in SI~Sup.~K.

\section{Discussion}
The audit yields three findings organized around the question of whether the publicly documented recipe is sufficient to reproduce the released SLRTP2025 BT evaluator and its behavior on a constructed replay probe.

\emph{RQ1---Failed public-artifact reconstruction.} The released evaluator achieves a combination of training-data fit and in-domain generalization that no checkpoint constructed from the public recipe reproduces. Fourteen recipe-conditioned reconstructions reach development-set free-decode BLEU 6.5--9.4 versus the released 13.38, and training-set teacher-forced token accuracy 55--60\% versus 96.5\% (Table~\ref{tab:sanity_stats}). The training-pool free-decode readout is 78.8 BLEU (70.7\% EM) versus a family max of 9.3. A small-sample overfit test (BLEU reaching 100.0 on 50--100 samples) and a unified-inference regression test (all vocabs byte-identical across checkpoints; released REC reproduced at 12.78) rule out gross failures in loading, masking, and decoding but do not validate the full-scale training implementation.

Teacher-forced development-set NLL is actually \emph{lower} for the reconstructions (2.63--2.68 vs.\ released 3.24), consistent with less overfitting, but their teacher-forced token accuracy is comparable on dev (45.8--47.4\% vs.\ 48.6\%). The gap between released and reconstructed free-decode BLEU is therefore present in both training and development data and is already visible in teacher-forced token-level metrics, not only in free-decoding. These five converging descriptive comparisons (Table~\ref{tab:diagnostic_summary}): gap-competence, pass-through ratio, training-pool readout, frame-reversal loss, and the competence gate, all place the released evaluator far outside any constructible range. The reconstruction failure is not explained by a gross code-path defect (small-sample overfit and unified-inference regression tests pass) but by the gap between the public artifacts and the original training procedure, which we could not fully characterize without the unpublished training script.

\emph{RQ2---Non-reproduction of the stress-test ordering.} The fourteen recipe-conditioned reconstructions (shared architecture, tokenizer, manifest, selection rule; seeds 101--1405) give a seed-level PURE--REC gap prediction interval of $[-1.94,-0.46]$. The six pre-specified primary seeds alone yield $[-2.48,-0.28]$, all fourteen leave-one-seed-out folds keep the interval below zero, and a two-level seed$\times$item bootstrap CI is $[-1.49,-0.93]$ (\S\ref{sec:what-drives-reversal}). Twenty-four heterogeneous-family checkpoints (config-faithful, step-faithful, confirmation, long-schedule, rescue, distillation students, and ladder fractions; all decoded with the canonical donor registry) have strictly negative gaps (range $[-2.01,-0.21]$ per family, $[-2.01,-0.77]$ for the twelve distillation students; SI~Sup.~D). All counts are run counts after SHA-256 deduplication: 43 decoded runs give 42 unique weight binaries, and 38 non-degenerate runs (37 unique) all have strictly negative gaps (\S\ref{sec:method}). Cross-evaluator re-decoding of the noisy-gloss retrieval pipeline output (a deployment-realistic analogue of TN-PURE-v1; 641 PHX-public keys, SI~Table~S32) yields a released-evaluator gap of $-0.13$ (noisy-gloss 12.65 vs.\ REC 12.78) and 38/38 negative gaps across non-degenerate evaluators.

Because all constructible checkpoints are less competent than the released evaluator (decoded family max dev BLEU-4 9.8, training-log max 10.02, vs.\ 13.38), this non-reproduction is scoped to the public recipe and does not constitute a causal checkpoint-identity claim. Two local-robustness checks on the released checkpoint (Gaussian weight noise and fine-tuning from released weights, SI~Sup.~F) confirm the reversal is locally robust but probe only the vicinity of the released weights. OLS/GP extrapolation from the 42 unique non-released binaries is treated as exploratory visualization (SI~Sup.~K) because non-linear behavior inside the unobserved $(9.8, 13.38)$ interval cannot be excluded. Our rebuilt USTC-MoE output (SI~Table~S33) is pipeline-faithful but not score-faithful (2.42 vs.\ official 12.06) and should not be taken as representative of the original competition entry. The noisy-gloss result provides the primary realistic-system evidence.

\emph{RQ3---Reference-frame sensitivity and donor-origin coupling.} On the matched 461-item confidence=1 subset of Czehmann et al.'s~\cite{czehmann2026sacred} human back-translations, reference replacement attenuates the gap by 90.5\% ($+10.03 \to +0.95$, paired bootstrap CI $[7.44,10.86]$ absolute, $[0.81,1.01]$ ratio). Because the source-conditioned retriever selects donors by lexical overlap with the German source sentence, which is identical to the original reference text in PHX-public (\S\ref{sec:retrieval}), the attenuation reflects both reference-text information loss and disruption of the structural lexical coupling between the probe and the original reference. The asymmetric PURE-vs-REC drop under reference replacement ($1.31\times$ BLEU-4; $1.29\times$ full-641) is numerically inconsistent with simple symmetric floor compression, but BLEU's non-linearity and the coupling structure preclude a definitive floor-effect null-hypothesis test. Our contribution is applying the matched-subset protocol to the released evaluator and a frozen replay probe, not re-establishing reference defects that Czehmann et al. already documented. The donor-pool substitution (\S\ref{sec:exposure_results}) reverses the gap sign (UNSEEN-PURE-v1 scores 8.51 vs.\ REC 12.78, gap $-4.27$), and the factorial interaction between pool origin and donor similarity ($+3.70$) confirms that seen donors benefit from both provenance and similarity; because seen donors remain more similar than unseen within strata, this is a provenance contrast, not an identified training-exposure effect.

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.}
These findings extend prior critiques of BT validity from output faithfulness to public-recipe reproducibility: improved BT scores need not imply target conditioning \cite{hong2026conditional}, low recorded-pose BT baselines are documented \cite{walsh2024datadriven}, and BT likelihood is a more stable correlate of human judgment than decoded BLEU \cite{jiang2025meaningful}. Our reversal is a \emph{decoded-sacreBLEU} reversal --- teacher-forced BT likelihood does not reproduce it (REC NLL $3.060$ vs PURE $3.479$) --- and whole-donor replay is a constructed probe, whereas SOKE-style word-level retrieval can improve pose quality under a different representation \cite{soke}.

\paragraph{Value of the audit infrastructure despite the competence confound.}
Because the competence-matched condition is unmet, RQ2's non-reproduction result cannot be attributed to a causal checkpoint effect; it could reflect the competence gap, an unrecovered training mechanism, or their interaction. This constrains what the audit \emph{infers} but not what it \emph{provides}. The evidence infrastructure the audit contributes is independent of the competence confound and has lasting value for the language-resource and SLP communities.

First, the provenance table (Table~\ref{tab:provenance}) and three-way data reconciliation establish a verified ground truth for any future PHX-public BT-evaluator study: the released pose materialization differs from the official PHOENIX-2014T split by 41 IDs, and the released evaluator's manifest matches the former, not the latter. Second, the checkpoint registry with SHA-256 deduplication (SI~Sup.~D) demonstrates a standard for BT-evaluator auditing that prevents double-counting and enables exact third-party verification. Third, the unified-inference regression test (\S\ref{sec:sanity_checks}) provides a reusable protocol for isolating code-path defects from recipe insufficiency in any learned-metric reproducibility audit. Fourth, the leakage controls (pose permutation, label permutation, held-out signer; SI~Sup.~M) are an extensible diagnostic suite for learned evaluators on any dataset. Fifth, the single canonical materialization (SHA-256 \texttt{9170a530}) with its four-layer reproduction mechanism (number recomputation, analysis re-execution, model re-evaluation, and from-scratch retraining; \S\ref{sec:method}) is a concrete model for reproducible benchmarking infrastructure. These contributions do not depend on resolving the competence confound and are directly transferable to future audits of learned evaluation metrics on other language resources.

\paragraph{Actionable recommendations for benchmark organizers.}
The audit yields three transferable lessons, scoped to the constructed replay-probe stress case on PHX-public rather than to general SLP leaderboard stability.

\emph{Lesson 1: a released evaluator is a research artifact, not an infrastructure component.} The released \texttt{config.yaml} alone was insufficient for 70 reconstruction runs across 13 families to reach competence parity, and the readout pattern could not be localized to a training stage because intermediate checkpoints are unpublished. Benchmarks should release (i) the exact training command and seeds, (ii) data pipeline scripts beyond the manifest, (iii) a SHA-256 manifest of training tensors, (iv) intermediate checkpoints at least every 100 epochs, and (v) the fixed per-team pose outputs alongside the public scores; the current release provides only the Progressive Transformer baseline output, so rank-stability audits across independently trained evaluators cannot be reproduced by third parties on real competition systems.

\emph{Lesson 2: on template-dense benchmarks, retrieval entries may benefit from donor-exclusion disclosure.} A donor-exclusion sweep is cheap and exposes the retrieval advantage on PHX-public's template-dense register; retrieval-based leaderboard entries \emph{may} voluntarily attach $\tau \in \{0.3, 0.5\}$ donor-exclusion results as a transparency practice on template-dense benchmarks. The training-pool free-decode BLEU ratio (released: $\text{78.8}\,/\,\text{13.38}\approx5.9$; reconstructions: 0.93--1.12) and input-side pose permutation with output-equivariance verification (SI~Sup.~M) are two descriptive probes we found informative in this case study; whether they generalize to other benchmarks is untested.

\emph{Lesson 3: reference-coupling artifacts warrant a supplementary reference check.} On PHX-public, the 90.5\% attenuation under Czehmann et al.~\cite{czehmann2026sacred} back-translations is a single-annotator reference-frame case study showing that the gap is sensitive to the reference frame; it should not be generalized beyond this signer's interpretation. Benchmarks on similarly template-dense corpora could adopt human back-translations as a supplementary reference set, follow the matched-subset protocol, and flag systems whose gap shifts between the two; the cost is one reference file and a paired bootstrap. Whether this generalizes beyond PHX-public's lexical-coupling structure is untested.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The audit is confined to PHX-public, a template-dense weather domain with documented reference-validity and train--test-overlap issues~\cite{czehmann2026sacred,alkain2026critical}. The fixed nine-system stress-test set comprises replay, composition, weak generation, and random controls rather than competitive SLP systems. The public leaderboard (\S\ref{sec:competition_real}) offers only single-evaluator context. Per-team pose outputs are not archived; our two fixed-output cross-evaluator studies (the noisy-gloss retrieval pipeline and the official winner's outputs rebuilt from its released intermediates) decode at or below REC under all 38 non-degenerate evaluators, but the USTC-MoE rebuild is not score-faithful to the archived submission (2.42 vs.\ the official leaderboard score of 12.06), so no causal claim about evaluator retrieval-favoring follows.

All 67 non-holdout checkpoints are less competent than the released evaluator (decoded family max dev BLEU-4 9.8, training-log max 10.02, vs.\ 13.38). The original training history is unpublished, so competence-matched replication from the public recipe was not possible. Local-robustness checks (Gaussian weight noise and fine-tuning from the released checkpoint, SI~Sup.~F) confirm the reversal is not a fragile singular point but do not narrow the unobserved competence interval. Saunders et al.~\cite{saunders2021continuous} limited their rank-stability claim to similar configurations, which our competence-unmatched family does not directly test. The mechanism is not identifiable from public artifacts: neither recipe-conditioned reconstruction, rescue sweeps, config-faithful retraining, nor any tested distillation procedure transfers the property. The donor-pool substitution decomposition is path-dependent, and pool-origin contrasts are descriptive associations, not identified training-exposure effects.

The human back-translations are a single-annotator derivative resource without second-annotator adjudication. Czehmann et al.'s confidence flags partition the full 641 items into 461 confidence=1, 121 confidence=0.5, and 59 confidence=0.0 tiers. Restricting to confidence=1 (our matched-subset protocol) is conservative for the BLEU analysis, because lower-confidence items have shorter, more fragmented back-translations that would inflate the floor effect. It does discard 180 items (28\%), however, and a single annotator's confidence ratings are not independently validated. We cannot estimate inter-annotator agreement or quantify how a second independent back-translator would shift the attenuation. Qualitatively, the direction of the attenuation is stable across confidence tiers (confidence=0.5: gap $+13.3\to+4.1$, $69\%$ attenuation; confidence=0.0: gap $+6.7\to+0.2$, $97\%$ attenuation; SI~Table~S31), but the quantitative attenuation fraction varies with the subset, and the small sample sizes at lower confidence tiers preclude reliable estimation. The human-reference protocol should be viewed as a single-annotator case study whose quantitative attenuation is conditional on one signer's interpretation. Its qualitative conclusion (that reference replacement substantially reduces the gap) is robust to subset choice, but the precise 90.5\% estimate should not be taken as a population parameter.

Finally, the reversal is a decoded-BT-text-metric reversal, not a sign-language quality reversal. Without target-conditioned DGS signer judgment or a validated sign-native pose metric, we cannot determine whether the retrieved donor motion conveys target-equivalent meaning. We explicitly do not claim that donor motion fails to express the target, nor that the reversal indicates dataset contamination or pose memorization.

\section{Conclusion}
This public-recipe sufficiency audit reports three findings, organized by research question, on a $+10.24$ BLEU-point retrieval--recorded-poses reversal under the released SLRTP2025 BT evaluator on PHX-public.

For RQ1, the publicly documented training recipe does not reproduce the released evaluator's competence. Fourteen recipe-conditioned reconstructions reach development-set free-decode BLEU 6.5--9.4 (vs.\ released 13.38) and training-set teacher-forced token accuracy 55--60\% (vs.\ released 96.5\%); a small-sample overfit test (BLEU 100.0 on 50--100 samples) and a unified-inference regression test rule out gross loading and decoding failures but do not validate the full-scale training implementation. The released evaluator's training-pool readout (78.8 BLEU, 70.7\% EM) is 8.5$\times$ above the family maximum.

For RQ2, the stress-test ordering does not reproduce: 38 non-degenerate decoded runs (37 unique weight binaries after SHA-256 deduplication) all yield negative PURE--REC gaps (reconstruction range $[-1.85,-0.80]$; heterogeneous-family ranges per family in SI~Sup.~D). Cross-evaluator re-decoding of a realistic noisy-gloss retrieval output yields 38/38 negative gaps across non-degenerate evaluators.

For RQ3, replacing original references with human back-translations reduces the gap from $+10.03$ to $+0.95$ on the matched 461-item confidence=1 subset (paired item CI $[-0.13, 1.99]$; residual not distinguishable from zero), and donor-origin substitution reverses its sign. The source-conditioned retriever shares surface-level vocabulary with the original reference (\S\ref{sec:retrieval}), so attenuation reflects both reference-text properties and disruption of this lexical coupling.

The scope is bounded. Local-robustness checks confirm the reversal is not a fragile singular point near the released checkpoint, but they probe only its vicinity; OLS/GP extrapolation from the 42 unique non-released binaries is exploratory because non-linear behavior inside the unobserved competence interval $(9.8, 13.38)$ cannot be excluded. Because competence-matched replication was not possible from public artifacts, the findings evidence a failed public-artifact reconstruction attempt, not a causal checkpoint effect, an identified memorization mechanism, or general leaderboard-rank instability.
