\section{Missing Test ID Sensitivity}
\label{sec:appendix_missing_id}
The official PHOENIX-2014T metadata contain 7,096/519/642 train/development/test IDs. The released SLRTP2025 pose records available to this study contain 7,060/515/641 sequences: 36/4/1 official IDs are absent from the released pose materialization. Their IDs and the reason \texttt{absent\_from\_slrtp2025\_released\_pose\_records} are enumerated in \texttt{artifact/manifests/exclusions.jsonl}. The missing test ID is \texttt{13December\_2010\_Monday\_tagesschau-6940}; its text/gloss metadata are not present in the released pose record, so length and template density cannot be reconstructed without an additional official annotation source. We therefore condition every result on the complete 641-record release rather than assert that the missing item is ignorable. As an empirical observed-extreme sensitivity scenario, assigning the missing item the per-item score of the largest PURE-over-REC outlier observed among the 641 released items changes the corpus-level gap by less than $\pm 0.2$ BLEU points, well within the reported 95\% paired-bootstrap interval $[+9.6,+12.4]$. This is an observed-extreme scenario, not a worst-case bound: corpus BLEU is non-linear and the missing item's reference, hypothesis, and n-gram sufficient statistics are unknown, so the true mathematical worst case is not covered. All primary conclusions are therefore restricted to the released 641-record materialization.

\paragraph{Seed-stratified dispersion intervals and two-level resampling.}
The 14-seed within-implementation seed dispersion interval splits into the six internally prespecified primary seeds and the eight post-hoc extension seeds: the primary-six give a Student-$t$ interval of $[-2.48,-0.28]$ (mean $-1.38$, SD $0.40$, 0/6 positive) and the extension-eight $[-1.61,-0.54]$ (mean $-1.07$, SD $0.21$, 0/8 positive). Leave-one-seed-out refits keep the interval below zero in all 14 folds (lower bounds $-1.99$ to $-1.80$; upper bounds $-0.51$ to $-0.42$). A two-level bootstrap resamples seeds with replacement (14 draws), then resamples each drawn seed's 641 items with replacement and averages the corpus gap over the 14 drawn seeds, giving a 95\% CI of $[-1.49,-0.93]$ (10{,}000 resamples, seed 42; per-item BLEU sufficient statistics precomputed once per seed, so the resamples are numerically identical to recomputing corpus BLEU per draw). Every seed-level resampling leaves the non-replication conclusion unchanged.

\paragraph{Discovery timeline.}
The $+10.24$ canonical reversal was first observed on 2026-07-24 in the materialization with exact-normalized-text exclusion (SHA-256 \texttt{9170a530}). An earlier pre-exclusion materialization (gap $+11.01$, PURE 23.79) had been noted on 2026-07-22; it survives only as a sensitivity analysis (Sup.~N). The Jaccard-based source-conditioned retriever, PURE whole-donor-copy protocol, exact-normalized-text exclusion rule, and 641-query set were locked before the fourteen reconstruction training runs began. Approximately four retrieval strategies (Jaccard, LaBSE, multi-SBERT, oracle gloss) and three construction variants (pure copy, PT-composed, oracle-gloss-composed) were explored on the released evaluator alone before the probe was frozen. No external pre-registration exists for the specific $+10.24$ probe as a confirmatory hypothesis. Because the Jaccard pure-copy probe was selected after exploring multiple retrievers and construction variants on the same test set (see the probe multiplicity table in main \S\ref{sec:probe_multiplicity}), its CI is conditional on the selection and should be read as descriptive, not confirmatory. The six primary reconstruction seeds (101--606) constitute the only pre-specified evidence: they were specified before their training results were inspected. The eight extension seeds (707--1405), the 24 heterogeneous-family checkpoints, the cross-evaluator re-decoding studies, the distillation ladder, and the extrapolation analyses were added post-hoc. Throughout, any analysis that uses the six-primary-seed set alone is labelled confirmatory (C); all analyses that pool across primary and extension seeds or use heterogeneous-family checkpoints are labelled descriptive (D). The evidence-grading column in Table~\ref{tab:si_provenance} reflects this distinction.

\paragraph{Readout--competence association.}
Thirty-eight constructible checkpoints plus the released evaluator have a uniform full-pool beam-3 free-decode readout (SI~Sup.~E protocol; the set of 31 used in earlier rounds plus the eight faithful readouts). Table~\ref{tab:readout_assoc} reports the Spearman correlations recomputed over this current set (\texttt{results/readout\_gap\_correlation.json}; \texttt{rescue\_wd0} has a uniform readout but no canonical gap and enters the readout--dev correlation only). Within the constructible family the readout is associated with decoded dev BLEU-4 ($\rho{=}0.86$--$0.88$ over the checkpoints that also have uniform dev decodes, $n{=}17$--$18$), but the readout--gap association is \emph{negative} ($\rho{=}{-}0.56$ excluding the released evaluator; $-0.44$ including it; $-0.44$ over non-degenerate constructible checkpoints): better-reading-out constructible checkpoints have \emph{more} negative gaps. The released evaluator is the single positive-gap checkpoint of the canonical (clip$=$1.0) family and lies outside the family trend on both axes (family readout range $0$--$12.08$ vs.\ $78.8$; family train-minus-dev range $-0.7$ to $+0.9$ vs.\ $65.4$), so neither a tracking nor an overfitting interpretation of its readout-with-generalization signature is supported by any within-family association. These within-regime facts must not be extrapolated across clipping regimes: the sensitivity grid of Sup.~U shows the readout--gap relation turning positive once unclipped training enters the memorisation regime (readout 99.98, gap $+11.19$), resolving the co-occurrence as a clipping-regime property rather than an uncovered region. Teacher-forced correlations are computed over the 15 checkpoints with checkpoint-level stats (14 reconstructions plus the released evaluator): readout correlates negatively with train NLL ($\rho{=}{-}0.561$, $p{=}0.030$) and positively with train token accuracy ($\rho{=}0.509$, $p{=}0.052$).

\section{Config-Faithful and Perturbation Details}
\label{sec:appendix_config_faithful}

\paragraph{Released \texttt{config.yaml} to training-code mapping (four-column, defect-corrected).}

\paragraph{Perturbation battery.}
\label{sec:appendix_perturbation}
Table~\ref{tab:perturbation} reports the full pose-perturbation battery. \paragraph{Released-checkpoint weight-noise perturbation (experiment A1).}
The reviewer's competence confound (released dev BLEU-4 13.38 vs.\ reconstructions $\leq 9.4$ under the uniform full-pool beam-3 protocol) cannot be broken by retraining. Starting from the released weights, we add i.i.d.\ Gaussian noise to every parameter (excluding BatchNorm running statistics, which must remain positive) at scale $\sigma\in\{0, 10^{-3}, 3\!\times\!10^{-3}, 10^{-2}, 3\!\times\!10^{-2}\}$ with two seeds each, and re-decode PHX-public REC/PURE and the dev set under the canonical donor registry (Fig.~\ref{fig:s2_released_perturbation}). Small $\sigma$ perturbs checkpoint identity while preserving competence, so this design operates where the 14-seed reconstruction family cannot --- at and below the released competence from the released checkpoint itself.

The reversal is preserved under competence-preserving perturbation and collapses in lockstep with competence. At $\sigma\leq 3\!\times\!10^{-3}$ the dev BLEU-4 stays at $12.50$--$13.38$ (above every reconstruction's uniform maximum of $10.5$) and the PURE--REC gap stays at $+9.50$ to $+10.35$ (within $\pm 0.75$ of the baseline $+10.24$). The collapse happens only at $\sigma\geq 10^{-2}$, where dev BLEU-4 simultaneously degrades to $7.3$--$10.2$ (gap $+3.2$ to $+4.2$), and at $\sigma=3\!\times\!10^{-2}$ both dev BLEU-4 ($\approx 0.1$) and the gap ($\approx 0$) are extinguished together. Two conclusions follow: the reversal is not a fragile identity-level artifact under small-weight perturbations (at competence parity it is locally robust), and it tracks competence rather than checkpoint identity. However, Gaussian noise probes only the isotropic weight-norm ball around the released checkpoint; it does not show that an independently trained, competence-matched model would exhibit the reversal, nor does it narrow the unobserved competence interval above the family ceiling of 10.5 (joint-loss beam-3 / step-corrected max under the uniform protocol).

\paragraph{Released-checkpoint fine-tuning (experiment A2).}
Gaussian weight noise probes only the isotropic neighbourhood; to test local robustness along genuine gradient directions, we fine-tune the released checkpoint with small fixed learning rates (5e-6, 1e-5, 3e-5) for 60 epochs, 3 seeds each (9 runs total), using the identical training data and architecture as the released evaluator. Models are selected by best dev NLL and decoded under the canonical donor registry (beam-3). All fine-tuned variants preserve the reversal (gaps $+10.37$ to $+10.77$, all within $\pm 0.5$ of baseline $+10.24$, dev BLEU-4 $12.92$--$13.18$, all above the reconstruction ceiling of $10.5$ under the uniform protocol) and retain the training-pool readout signature (train BLEU $80.5$--$81.8$, EM $72.6$--$74.0\%$, vs.\ baseline $78.8$/$70.7\%$). Results are in \texttt{results/released\_finetune.json}. In contrast to isotropic noise, fine-tuning moves weights along data-driven gradient directions near the released optimum; the persistence of both the reversal and the readout signature under fine-tuning confirms they are not fragile singular-point properties. The same scope limit applies: like the Gaussian noise experiment, fine-tuning probes only the vicinity of the released weights and does not narrow the unobserved interval above the family ceiling (10.5).

\paragraph{Epoch-checkpoint decoupling search (experiment D).}

\section{Supplementary Oracle Diagnostics}
\label{sec:appendix_oracle}
\paragraph{Source-conditioned retrieval is legal, oracle-gloss retrieval is leakage.}
SLRTP2025 supplies spoken-language source text as legal inference input. Under pure whole-donor copy, source-text Jaccard scores 23.02 sacreBLEU (0--100 scale, as throughout this appendix), LaBSE 19.2, and multi-SBERT 16.5; the PT-composed source-text condition scores 18.86 (see main text, Table~\ref{tab:probe_multiplicity}). Oracle-gloss retrieval uses reference gloss and is leakage, whereas noisy-gloss retrieval uses predicted gloss. Thus the strongest legal source-conditioned result is the 23.02 whole-donor stress test, which uses no generator or scaffold.
\paragraph{Disclosure of selection/evaluation reuse.}
The white-box oracle analyses in this appendix reuse the same target reference and evaluator for both donor selection and decoded evaluation. This is an intentional adversarial design: diagonal entries quantify susceptibility to a selector that knows its evaluator, not an independent validation of retrieval quality.

\paragraph{Cross-checkpoint transfer matrix.}
For each query, a selector maximizes its evaluator's teacher-forced likelihood over candidate donor poses; the selected fixed pose is then decoded by every evaluator (token-normalized NLL, sorted donor IDs, \texttt{numpy.argmax} first tie-break). All seven diagonal margins are positive (mean $+0.0150$, 95\% paired-bootstrap CI $[+0.0105,+0.0198]$). Original-selected donors transfer to five of six reconstructions (mean local-REC margin $+0.0074$, $[+0.0016,+0.0135]$); the reverse direction fails in all six cells (reconstruction-selected donors evaluated by Original, mean margin $-0.0455$ $[-0.0554,-0.0358]$). The full 7$\times$7 matrix, unrounded cells, margins, and CIs are in the artifact.



\paragraph{Candidate-pool size.}
The Original-selector stress test shows a strong search-budget effect: at 8, 32, 128, 512 and 2,048 candidates, decoded sacreBLEU remains below local REC by $-12.1$, $-9.2$, $-5.7$, $-3.6$ and $-1.3$ BLEU points respectively; only the full 7,060-donor pool is detectably positive ($+1.7$, $[+0.4,+3.0]$).

\paragraph{Reference-overlap exclusion.}
Masking 1,036 exact-text query--donor pairs before selection: Original-on-Original decoded BLEU is 13.86 versus local REC 12.78 (absolute difference $+1.08$ BLEU points), but the paired relative-margin 95\% CI crosses zero; the frozen joint likelihood-and-interval success rule is met in 0/7 checkpoints. Masking all 19,694 pairs with source-token Jaccard $\geq 0.5$: the score falls to 8.30 ($-4.47$), again 0/7 meet the rule. The full-pool diagonal advantage does not survive as a statistically robust decoded-BLEU result after exact-text exclusion and disappears after high-overlap exclusion.



\paragraph{Noisy-gloss retrieval is split-dependent.}
A 6-layer text$\to$gloss Transformer (dev BLEU-4 13.17) yields noisy-gloss retrieval sacreBLEU 12.7 on PHX-public (strong-NMT), versus 1.6 for a weak NMT (BLEU-4 1.15) and 6.1 for XLM-R embedding retrieval; donor exclusion reduces the strong-NMT value to 10.9 at $\tau{=}0.5$, and the same control reaches 0.4 on the template-disjoint holdout (Table~\ref{tab:holdout_controls}).

\paragraph{Caveats.}
First, the GP upper bound ($+0.3$) partly reflects RBF kernel prior shrinkage toward the mean in the extrapolation region (sparse data beyond dev BLEU-4 10.5), not purely data evidence. Second, the OLS quadratic fit is sensitive to the distillation family and to the inclusion of the degenerate checkpoints. Third, the 61 non-degenerate checkpoints are not IID. We therefore report these fits only as descriptive visualizations and explicitly avoid claiming that ``competence alone is unlikely to explain the reversal'' on the basis of these fits. The unobserved $(10.5, 13.38)$ interval could in principle harbor non-linear behavior that the present experiments cannot rule out.

\paragraph{Per-item gap decomposition (experiment C).}
To characterize the \emph{mechanism} rather than the identity question, we regress the per-item sentence-level PURE--REC gap $y_i$ (sacreBLEU sentence BLEU-4 under the released evaluator, canonical donor registry; mean $8.4$, SD $18.7$, $n=641$) on interpretable item-level features: donor--reference token-set Jaccard, normalized character Levenshtein between donor and reference, an exact-match-train-text flag, the number of training texts sharing the reference's 60-character prefix (template density), the number of training texts with Jaccard $\geq 0.5$ to the reference (high-overlap-train), reference text length, donor reuse count, a confidence=1 flag, and a 9-level signer one-hot block (Fig.~\ref{fig:s3_per_item_decomp}). The full model explains $R^2=0.33$ of the per-item variance. The strongest single-feature contributions, measured by leave-one-out $R^2$ drop, are high-overlap-train ($0.093$), template density ($0.051$), and donor--reference Levenshtein ($0.039$); the donor--reference Jaccard contributes $0.007$, the exact-match flag $0.001$, and the entire signer block $0.008$ --- signer identity is essentially irrelevant.

The signs of the standardized coefficients are informative. Items where the donor is lexically close to the reference (high Levenshtein similarity, $\beta^*=+6.1$; high donor--reference Jaccard, $\beta^*=+2.9$) show \emph{larger} gaps, whereas items with many high-overlap training neighbors ($\beta^*=-7.6$) or high template density ($\beta^*=-7.3$) show \emph{smaller} gaps. The reversal is therefore largest on items where the retrieval criterion (source-text Jaccard) happens to deliver a donor that matches the reference register closely, but where the recorded poses do not independently let the recognizer recover that text --- precisely the lexical-coupling mechanism predicted by the construction (the original reference is a speech transcription of the same source sentence the donor was selected by). Signer identity and text length contribute negligibly; the unexplained $67\%$ of variance is consistent with per-item pose variability and BT-decoder stochasticity that these surface features do not capture. This decomposition does not identify a hidden-pipeline detail; it localizes the reversal to a lexical-coupling property that is constructible on PHX-public's template-dense, high train--test overlap register, consistent with the corpus-level audit of Alkain et al.~\cite{alkain2026critical}.

\section{Reconstruction Sanity Checks (Reviewer Round-2)}
\label{sec:appendix_sanity}
This appendix documents the three sanity checks requested by the reviewer to rule out a systematic implementation defect shared across all 68 training runs. Scripts: \texttt{scripts/e\_overfit\_test.py}, \texttt{scripts/e\_checkpoint\_stats.py}, \texttt{scripts/e\_inference\_regression.py}. Results: \texttt{results/overfit\_test.json}, \texttt{results/checkpoint\_stats.json}, \texttt{results/inference\_regression.json}.

\paragraph{Small-sample overfit test.}
A reconstruction model was trained on 50 (and separately 100) randomly sampled training sequences for $\approx$200 epochs using the identical pipeline as the paper-derived reconstruction runs. On the 50-sample set, teacher-forced NLL fell from 7.66 to 0.009 and free-decode sacreBLEU-4 reached 100.00 under beam-3 decoding. On the 100-sample set, free-decode BLEU also reached 100.00 (300 epochs). The reconstruction code can therefore overfit small data to near-perfect free-decode BLEU, ruling out a fundamental defect in checkpoint loading, tokenizer, padding-mask, EOS, loss normalization, or the beam-search decoding path.

\paragraph{Unified inference regression.}
Released and reconstruction checkpoints are loaded through the identical \texttt{make\_back\_translation\_model()} function and decoded with the identical \texttt{back\_translate()} pipeline. All 15 checkpoints (released + 14 reconstructions) share byte-identical \texttt{txt.vocab} and \texttt{gls.vocab} files (SHA-256 confirmed). The released checkpoint decoded through this path reproduces the canonical REC value exactly (12.78 on 641 PHX-public items). The tested reconstruction seeds (101, 202, 303) decode REC at 9.24, 8.22, 9.10 respectively, matching the main-text table. Inference code path is identical for all checkpoints.

\section{Frozen-Split Confirmation and Reference-Free Retrieval}
\label{sec:appendix_frozen_split}

\paragraph{Dev-split confirmation of the headline probe.}
The canonical probe panel was constructed and explored on the 641-item PHX-public \emph{test} split; the probe response was therefore confirmed on the development split (515 items), which was never used for probe construction or exploration. The identical TN-PURE-v1 construction (NFKC normalization, token-set Jaccard, character-Levenshtein then SHA-256 tie-breaks, exact-normalized-text exclusion, donors from the same 7{,}060-item training pool) was applied with dev items as queries, and both REC (recorded dev poses) and PURE (donor poses) were decoded under the released evaluator with the canonical beam-3 protocol. The released evaluator's dev REC BLEU-4 is 13.379, matching the 13.38 logged in its released \texttt{validations.txt} to three digits and independently validating the decode pipeline. The dev-split PURE score is 21.47, a gap of $+8.10$ versus the test-split $+10.24$ (REC 13.38 / PURE 21.47 vs.\ 12.78 / 23.02). The reversal therefore reproduces, with consistent direction and comparable magnitude, on a split untouched by probe exploration. Per-item files and the dev donor registry (SHA-256 recorded in the result JSON) are in \texttt{results/dev\_split\_confirmation.json} and \texttt{results/dev\_split\_confirmation\_items/}.

\paragraph{Pose-retrieval probe: donor selection without the scored reference.}
The text-based retrievers (Jaccard, LaBSE, multi-SBERT) all select donors using the query's text---which is identical to the BLEU scoring reference (target leakage by construction). To sever this channel while retaining a meaningful retrieval signal, donors were selected by \emph{pose} similarity to the query's own recorded pose sequence, an input-side signal available to a deployed system: each sequence (after the canonical 12.5\,fps subsampling) is summarised by its time-averaged pose and mean absolute frame-to-frame velocity, each part z-normalized per dimension over the pooled queries and donors and L2-normalized, and the donor minimising the combined Euclidean distance is selected (ties by donor-ID hash, the same final tie-break convention as the canonical retriever; no text is used anywhere in the loop). Under the released evaluator on the same 641-item test split, POSE-PURE scores 0.45 (gap $-12.33$ vs.\ REC 12.78), indistinguishable from the random-donor baseline (0.90, gap $-11.88$) and far below text-retrieval TN-PURE (23.02, $+10.24$). The positive probe response is therefore \emph{entirely dependent} on selecting donors with the scored reference text: any reference-free selection rule examined (random, pose-similarity) collapses to deeply negative gaps. Implementation and per-item files: \texttt{scripts/e\_pose\_retrieval\_probe.py}, \texttt{results/pose\_retrieval\_probe.json}, \texttt{results/pose\_retrieval\_probe\_items/}.

\section{Gradient-Clipping and CTC-Normalisation Sensitivity (Sup.~U)}
\label{sec:appendix_clip_sensitivity}
\textbf{Question answered:} are the training-pool readout signature and the positive probe response unrecoverable properties of the released evaluator, or artefacts of the two disclosed training-dynamics inferences (gradient-clip threshold; CTC-term normalisation)?

\textbf{Design.} Seed-42 sensitivity runs of the faithful protocol (\texttt{src/training/train\_faithful.py}) varying one knob each: CTC denominator (sentences / gloss tokens / input frames / raw sum) at clip 1.0; gradient-clip \texttt{max\_norm} (1.0 = the faithful run, 2.0, 3.0, 5.0, none $=\infty$); and a signjoey-exact run (no clipping $+$ CTC raw sum, the literal \texttt{\_train\_batch} semantics of the underlying framework). All runs use the released seed-42 init, the released config otherwise, and the \texttt{--stop-frozen} termination (LR frozen at the plateau scheduler's numerical floor with the best checkpoint unchanged); none belongs to the canonical 76-run panel (registry directories \texttt{checkpoints/ctc\_clip\_sens/}, \texttt{checkpoints/faithful\_steps/}). Evaluation: canonical 641-query REC/PURE protocol, full 7{,}060-item training-pool free-decode readout with exact-match, via \texttt{scripts/eval\_step\_ckpts.py} into \texttt{results/faithful\_steps\_eval.json}.

\textbf{Source evidence for no clipping.} The released repository reuses the signjoey (\texttt{neccam/slt}) pipeline: its \texttt{validations.txt} column layout matches signjoey's \texttt{\_add\_report} line format field-for-field, and the data/model code paths correspond. In signjoey's \texttt{training.py}, \texttt{build\_gradient\_clipper} inspects the config fields \texttt{clip\_grad\_val} and \texttt{clip\_grad\_norm}; when neither is present it returns \texttt{None}, and \texttt{\_train\_batch} then applies no clipping (when present, the framework clips by \emph{value}, not by norm). The released \texttt{config.yaml} contains neither field, so the framework-default reading of the release is that the released training ran unclipped. The same source pins the CTC-term semantics: \texttt{\_train\_batch} divides the translation loss by \texttt{num\_seqs} (the \texttt{batch} normalization) but the recognition loss only by \texttt{batch\_multiplier} ($=$1), i.e.\ the raw CTC sum---the source carries an explicit developer comment (\texttt{\# TODO (Cihan): Add Gloss Token normalization (?)}) acknowledging the missing normalisation. Our \texttt{max\_norm}$=$1.0 was inherited from the v1--v3 scripts and disclosed as an author inference; this table overturns it. The inference chain is source-semantic (framework version assumption), not a released transcript; the behavioural confirmation is the ``none'' row reproducing both previously unreproduced properties. (signjoey's stock stopping logic---stop when a plateau decay fires and no improvement occurred at the pre-decay LR---would have terminated the released run at roughly the second post-best decay, a dozen validations after its step-1{,}820 best; the released run instead logged 72 further validations, so the release's own stopping rule remains externally terminated and the step-matched analysis of the main text applies.)

\textbf{Unclipped trajectory.} The unclipped seed-42 run improves dev BLEU-4 monotonically to 15.69 at the released run's own best step (1{,}820), reaches its best 16.32 at step 2{,}352, and continues to the scheduler's numerical floor (step 7{,}014, 501 validations, LR frozen at 4.4e-8) without the best checkpoint changing; it was then terminated externally (the same manual rule as the published faithful runs). Pre-clip gradient norms over the first ten optimiser steps (377, 568, 145, 33, \ldots) exceed the 1.0 threshold by up to two orders of magnitude, so the clipped and unclipped regimes diverge from the first update: at clip 1.0 every step is direction-preserving truncation, and the memorisation regime is never entered.

\textbf{Step-matched unclipped readout.} A fresh unclipped run with checkpoints fixed at the released run's own best (1{,}820) and final (2{,}828) steps shows the memorisation regime is entered \emph{early}: at step 1{,}820 the readout is already 99.94 BLEU / 99.7\% EM (dev 15.69, REC 14.64, PURE 26.32, gap $+11.68$); at step 2{,}828, 99.98 / 99.9\% (dev 15.80, gap $+11.49$). The overshoot relative to the released evaluator's step-1{,}820 readout of 78.8 is therefore not an artefact of training longer than the released run: under unclipped dynamics the training pool is essentially memorised before the released run's best checkpoint.

\textbf{Multi-seed confirmation.} Across seeds 42--44 the unclipped protocol gives dev 15.74--16.32, REC 14.32--15.96, gaps $+11.19$/$+11.31$/$+10.31$, and readouts 99.98/95.25/99.98 BLEU (90.5--99.9\% EM): the reproduction of both properties is stable across seeds, and every seed's gap is within 1 BLEU point of the released $+10.24$.

\textbf{Interpretation and caveats.} The readout signature and the probe response are clipping-regime properties of the released recipe, constructible by disabling clipping (or, at intermediate strength, dialled to the released magnitude: clip 5.0 gives readout 82.69/68.4\% vs.\ the released 78.8/70.7\%, the closest single match of the ladder). The released operating point is bracketed but not matched in all coordinates---no single threshold places dev 13.38, readout 78.8, REC 12.78, and gap $+10.24$ simultaneously---so at least one further quantitative difference (CTC normalisation, stopping point, framework version, or data-order effects) persists; because the released config implies no clipping, the mismatch indicates the released run's trajectory was slower than ours in dev-competence terms, not that it was clipped. The within-regime readout--gap association of the canonical panel ($\rho{=}{-}0.56$; Sup.~D) must not be extrapolated across regimes: across the ladder it is strictly positive (readout and gap rise together at every step from clip 1.0 to none).
